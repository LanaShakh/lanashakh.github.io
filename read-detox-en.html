A weekend digital detox: take a break from your phone and stay in touch

You open your phone to check the route and end up reading a work chat. You want to photograph the view from the terrace and linger in your feed. Your countryside break has begun, but your attention is still caught up in everything you left at home.

You want a different kind of weekend: long breakfasts, conversations, walks and time to notice the place you have come to see. Yet you still want to be available to your parents, children and anyone who might need you. That is what a weekend digital detox can be: giving your time to the trip itself and the people beside you.

At Na Krayu Zemli, a glamping retreat near Kozelsk, the day begins with a view of trees and the valley through a panoramic window. Outside is your chalet's terrace, with fields and woodland beyond. While the tea brews, you already want to step out, see where the path leads and find a spot for breakfast in the fresh air.

“I'm at the edge of the world. Literally.”

Here, that status or automatic reply is almost a location tag: Na Krayu Zemli means “at the edge of the world”. A short terrace video, a cup in the frame and a caption: “I'm at the edge of the world. Literally. I'll reply when I'm back. If it's urgent, call.” Your family knows where you are, and you already have an answer to “How is it over there?”

Agreeing that urgent matters deserve a call and muting work chats takes a little preparation, but gives you fewer reasons to watch every message. There is time for replies after breakfast or in the evening. Between them is a whole day you would rather spend here than describe minute by minute in a chat.

What you would rather do than scroll

Putting your phone in a bag is easier when there is something you want to put it away for. It might be the book you keep setting aside at home. Or a walk and a long story from your companion that you finally get to hear to the end. For someone else, driving a quad bike is much more interesting than watching another video of somebody else's adventure.

On a quad bike ride, your attention is on the route and what is happening around you. There is a trail ahead, companions beside you, and your own stories and photographs afterwards. At dinner, you have something to talk about besides the videos recommended to you that day. Discuss the available ride and arrangements with the manager when booking.

There is a different mood in our pottery workshop. Think of a shape, build up the sides and add details: your hands have a job, and you can see the result right in front of you. It is interesting to watch both your own piece and how the person beside you approaches the same task. You might start with similar ideas and finish with something quite different.

Between activities, it is lovely simply to walk around the grounds. The chalets are surrounded by fields, woods and a birch grove above the Seryona Valley. Take a book, stop at a view you like and return by a different path. This part of the day does not need a separate plan: you can already see where you want to go from your cabin.

Time together that is hard to find at home

At breakfast, conversations often stop halfway through: a message arrives, someone gets distracted and the subject is lost. Away from home, it feels good to finish the story, laugh at what happened next and remember something together. Breakfast on the terrace stretches out, the tea gets cold and nobody wants to be the first to leave the table.

For a family, a board game can become that shared time. A private chalet has room to sit together, lay out the board and cards, and stay for another round. The box you have not opened for months finally comes with you. Now everyone is paying attention: someone wants to beat the others, someone keeps making them laugh and someone is asking for a rematch.

There is time alone too. While one person reads by the window, another goes for a walk. At dinner, there are new things to talk about. The break holds both shared experiences and space for your own small pleasures.

A few photographs you will want to keep

A good photograph from a trip can be very simple: the person opposite you is laughing, sunlight falls across the table, the terrace lights have come on. You want to keep that moment. Then the next one happens: someone calls you for a walk, brings tea or continues the conversation.

Back in the chalet that evening, it is enjoyable to look through the pictures together. Everyone has their own version of the same day: your landscape, your companion's funny portrait of you, a detail your child noticed that the adults walked past. Choosing a few pictures for family and keeping the rest for an album is another reason to sit together.

Go away for the trip itself

You want the evening beside your chalet to last. First dinner on the terrace, then a soak in comfortably warm water as the sky over the valley darkens. Each cabin has its own furako, a Japanese-style hot tub. If you have been imagining an evening like this, see our guide to staying in a cabin with a hot tub.

These are the weekends you want to remember: your first piece of pottery, the excitement of a quad bike ride, the family rematch at the games table. Or a few finished chapters and a long conversation you finally had time for. Questions and answers

Is a digital detox worth it for just two days?

A short trip has a clear purpose: to spend the weekend paying more attention to yourself, the people you are with and your surroundings. Two days give you a chance to try a different rhythm and notice which activities interest you more than your feed. You do not have to wait for a long holiday to try it.

How can I take a phone break if my companion has different habits?

You can share some screen-free moments even if you use your phones differently. Breakfast together, for example, or a workshop you both enjoy. Personal time can remain personal: enjoying the trip does not require identical rules for everyone.

What might I want to keep doing when I get home?

Often, it is a particular activity you finally found time for: reading before bed, playing games as a family or enjoying a long breakfast together. The trip can continue in a small way at home. Something you enjoyed becomes another reason to put the phone aside.
